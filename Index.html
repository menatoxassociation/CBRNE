01  Definition

Hazardous materials

Anything that injures people, property or the environment when it is released, by accident or on purpose.

Emergency planners sort it into five letters. The categories differ in how fast they act, how patients arrive and who leads the response, but they end at the same door: yours.

What it harmsPeople, property, environment
How it is releasedBy accident or on purpose
How it is sortedC · B · R · N · E

03  Radiological

Exposed is not contaminated

The distinction decides whether a radiation casualty needs decontamination before entering your department, and whether staff are at any risk from the patient.

Exposed

The patient was irradiated by a source but carries no radioactive material. Once away from the source, the patient poses no radiation hazard to staff. Treat like any other patient.

Contaminated

Radioactive material is on the skin or clothing, or inside the body. Decontaminate, starting with removing clothing, and contain what comes off. Life-threatening injuries still come first.

Protection from any source rests on three words:

TimeLess time near it
DistanceMore distance from it
ShieldingSomething between you and it

04  What the five share

The patient is the scene

They reach your emergency department before anyone calls you.

Whatever the letter, the first minutes at the door follow the same four moves. Tap each one as you would run it.

  1. Appropriate personal protective equipment goes on before contact. A contaminated or unprotected clinician becomes a second patient and takes a team member out of the response.
  2. One way in. Patients from an incident are held and assessed outside the clinical area so contamination stays outside. Self-presenting patients will not wait for instructions.
  3. Removing clothing is the first and simplest step of decontamination. Bag and label it, and keep it with the patient's records.
  4. Support airway, breathing and circulation, and treat the clinical picture in front of you. The confirmed agent name often arrives late, or wrong.
